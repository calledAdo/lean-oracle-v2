---
title: Feed policy
description: How feeds are added, changed and retired, and what stays fixed.
---

## What never changes

- **A feed's ID.** It is derived from the symbol and never reused.
- **A feed's meaning.** `Crypto.BTC/USDT` is always a spot price of BTC in USDT from native markets.
  A different meaning, such as a time-weighted average, gets a new symbol
  (for example `Crypto.CKB/USDT.TWAP60`).
- **A feed's exponent.** `-8` for majors and USDT/USD, `-10` for CKB pairs.

## What can change

Exchanges, minimum exchange counts, spreads, outlier limits and other methodology settings. Changes go
through a new version of the committee config, which a quorum of publishers must sign and which takes
effect at a stated tick. Every signed update carries the hash of the config that produced it, so any
price can be traced to its exact settings.

## Adding a feed

A pair becomes a feed only if enough independent exchanges trade it natively:

- at least two exchanges must agree on every price;
- one more market than required must be listed, so one exchange failing does not stop the feed;
- each exchange appears once, with a public order book API, real volume and depth.

Adding a feed needs no transaction and doesn't affect existing feeds. USDC/USD is not a feed yet
because only two exchanges trade it directly, one short of these rules.

## Retiring a feed

A retired feed is simply no longer included in updates. Existing feed cells keep their last price and
can be burned. Retirements will be announced in the [changelog](/docs/reference/changelog) ahead of
time on mainnet.

## Missing ticks

If fewer than the required exchanges are healthy in a tick, that tick carries no price for the feed.
The mirror's `at` endpoint then returns the next tick that does. Plan a fallback for settlement.
