title: FAQ
description: Common questions about Lean Oracle.
---

### Is Lean Oracle on mainnet?

Not yet. It runs on CKB testnet with a single publisher. Mainnet follows once independent publishers
have joined and the contracts are audited.

### Do I have to update the feed cell myself?

Yes, or whoever your cell's lock allows. Nothing updates your cell for you. Most applications
update it in the same transaction that uses the price.

### Can I read someone else's feed cell?

Yes, as a cell dep, but check its committee type hash, and remember they control when it updates.
Owning your cell is simpler and safer.

### How fresh is a price?

A new update is signed every second (two for CKB). How old the price in your cell is depends on when
it was last updated. Your contract decides what is fresh enough; see
[Check freshness](/docs/build/guides/freshness).

### Why are BTC/USD and BTC/USDT different feeds?

They are priced from different markets and differ when USDT is off its peg. See
[native pairs](/docs/feeds#native-pairs-usd-usdt-and-usdc-are-different-feeds).

### What happens if the mirror goes down?

Your contract keeps its last price, and your application can't fetch new updates until a mirror is
back. Configure several mirror URLs, or run your own.

### What happens if publishers go down?

Updates continue as long as a quorum is up. If fewer are up, no new updates are signed until they
return; your freshness check stops your contract from using an old price.

### Can a mirror or relayer manipulate prices?

No. They can only choose among genuinely signed updates, and your cell only moves forward. Freshness
checks close the remaining gap: someone choosing a slightly older signed update.

### What does it cost?

Lean Oracle is free. You pay CKB storage for your feed cell and ordinary transaction fees. See
[Costs](/docs/learn/costs).

### How is this different from Pyth?

The model is similar: a pull oracle with signed updates served by a gateway. The differences are that
Lean Oracle is built for CKB's cell model, each project owns its own feed cell, and prices come from
Lean Oracle's own publisher committees rather than Pyth's publishers.
