title: Trust model
description: What you trust, what you don't, and what the oracle leaves to you.
---

## You trust the committee

A price is accepted when a quorum of the committee's current publishers signed it. You trust that
fewer than a quorum of publishers will collude or be compromised at once. Publishers are
permissioned and named in the committee cell.

<Callout type="warn" title="Testnet">
  The testnet committees currently have one publisher, so testnet prices rest on a single key.
</Callout>

## You don't trust the transport

| Party | Can | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror, CDN, cache | Delay or withhold updates | Forge or change a price |
| Whoever sends your update transaction | Choose which signed update to apply | Apply an older price than the cell holds, or a forged one |
| Anyone creating feed cells | Create a cell anchored to their own committee | Make your contract accept it, if it checks the committee |

## Changing the committee

Keys change only through a rotation of the committee cell, authorized by a quorum of the current
set, with proof that the new keys are held. After a rotation, only the new set's updates are
accepted. A committee can also be paused, which stops all updates.

## What the oracle leaves to you

- **Freshness.** Scripts cannot read the clock, and the oracle never rejects old prices. Your contract
  compares the publish time with a time it can prove.
- **When to update.** Nobody updates your feed cell but you (or whoever your lock allows).
- **Snapshots and settlement rules.** For example, which tick settles a market.
- **Cross rates and bounds.** How to combine feeds, and what confidence or deviation to accept.

See [Your responsibilities](/docs/build/responsibilities).

## The code

The contracts are immutable (`data2`, no upgrade key) and built reproducibly: anyone can rebuild them
and compare the code hashes with [Networks](/docs/reference/networks).
