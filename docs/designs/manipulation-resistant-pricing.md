# Design: Manipulation-resistant pricing (evidence-first)

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-27
Branch: design/pricing-methodology
Repo: calledAdo/lean-oracle-v2
Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

Make each publisher's price harder to manipulate, and base every threshold on recorded market data
rather than guesses. Today, per 1 s tick (apps/publisher/src/methodology.ts, aggregate.ts):
1. Each exchange is priced on its own:
   - `mid`: the median of top-of-book midpoints sampled every 100 ms over 1 s;
   - `vwap` (the CKB pairs): a 60 s trade VWAP, or `mid` if the venue had no trades.
2. Across exchanges: median, drop venues beyond `maxDeviationBps`, median again.
   `conf = max(median half-spread, MAD)`.
3. The leader takes the median of the publishers' prices.

This is a median of medians, the same family as Chainlink and Chronicle. Pyth differs: it weights
each publisher by the confidence it reports.

## What Makes This Cool

- An open oracle whose methodology changes are proven on replayed real market data. Every
  committee-config change can ship with "here is what it did to 24 h of real books".
- Later: an oracle that signs how much it would cost to move its own price. Nobody publishes attack
  cost on-chain today.

## Constraints

- The on-chain message format is frozen (v1 contract freeze). Every change is off-chain methodology,
  versioned and approved through the signed committee config.
- Publishers must stay deterministic. Given the same recorded inputs, two publishers produce
  identical observations.
- New derived feeds are new feed IDs (new Merkle leaves); no contract change.

## Premises (agreed, revised after live measurement)

Measured on CKB/USDT from the droplet, 2026-09-27:

| Venue | 24 h volume | Asks within 1% | Spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| Binance | $307k | $5.8k | 21 bps |
| MEXC | $60k | $6.3k | 24 bps |
| Gate | $34k | $2.4k | 5 bps |
| Bitget | $32k | $5.1k | 21 bps |
| KuCoin | $14k | $2.2k | 14 bps |

1. **Keep the median.**
   - An equal-weight median makes an attacker move 3 of 5 venues. The cheapest three need roughly
     $10k of buying to lift the median 1%.
   - An uncapped volume-weighted median would hand the price to Binance (69% of volume), and Binance
     alone moves 1% for about $6k. That is cheaper to attack.
   - If we ever weight venues, cap each one at a third.
2. **The hole is the 60 s VWAP, not spoofing.**
   - Spoofing the mid is bounded by the spread (5 to 25 bps), because orders that would cross the
     spread trade instead.
   - A 60 s trade VWAP on a thin venue is not bounded. KuCoin trades about $10 a minute, so wash
     prints at an off-market price own that venue's VWAP.
3. **The fix is off-chain methodology**, versioned by committee config.
4. **TWAP60 feeds need no contract change.** They are new feed IDs computed from the committee's
   finalized history (freeze plan D1, R15).
5. **The limit is depth.** No aggregation method rescues a feed whose best venues are thin.
   Consumers size risk against real depth.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Independent second opinion from a fresh Claude subagent (Codex was unavailable):
- **Challenged premise 1.** Venues are not independent. Arbitrage bots carry a Binance move to the
  other venues within seconds, so the real cost to move the median is below the sum of the three
  cheapest books. This is testable: in recorded data, measure how far and how fast other venues
  follow a 1% or larger Binance move. If they recover more than 70% of it within 5 s, time smoothing
  (TWAP) matters more than venue weighting.
- **Refined premise 2.** Venue trade timestamps and book snapshots are not synchronized. Compare
  each trade with the book as of the trade time, plus or minus a tolerance, widened by the spread.
- **Coolest version.** A signed `COST1PCT` feed: the notional needed to move the median 1%.
  Confidence that widens with thin depth and with disagreement between VWAP and mid.
- **Nearest code.** RedStone's open-source aggregation (deviation filters, VWAP and median,
  liquidity checks). Missing from it: trade-vs-book validation, depth-driven confidence, and a
  signed, versioned methodology with a replay harness proving publishers agree.
- **Recommendation.** Build the replay harness and the trade-vs-book filter first.

## Approaches Considered

- **A) Minimal filter.** The trade-vs-book filter and a VWAP volume floor, with unit tests only.
  Effort S. Rejected: every threshold would be a guess.
- **B) Evidence-first (chosen).** A, plus four things:
  - an in-process recorder;
  - a replay harness with determinism tests;
  - depth-aware conf;
  - the measurements that decide weighting and new feeds.

  Effort M, plus at least 3 days of recording. Trade-off: slower to ship than A, but every
  threshold is set from real books.
- **C) B plus signed risk feeds** (TWAP60, `COST1PCT`). Effort L. Deferred until B's data exists.
  TWAP also needs the settlement rules from the freeze plan designed first.

## Recommended Approach: B, evidence-first

### 1. Recorder (inside the live publisher)

- **Where it runs.** A `record` option in the publisher's operator config taps the same `MarketData`
  input the pricing uses: every quote, L2 depth within ±2%, trade and liveness event, with both
  timestamps (venue time and local receipt time).
- **What else it writes.** Each tick's own observations (price, conf, venues used). The replay
  harness can then be checked against exactly what the live publisher saw and signed.
- **Format.** Newline JSON per venue and market, rotated hourly and gzipped, in `dataDir/recordings`.
- **Disk budget.** `record.maxBytes` defaults to 3 GB. When a new hour would exceed it, the oldest
  files are deleted. With the storage-retention caps (mirror ~5.5 GB, publisher ~0.4 GB) the
  droplet stays under 60%.
- **Isolation.** Writes are asynchronous and best-effort. A full disk or a write error only turns
  recording off (with a log line and watchdog-visible health). It never blocks or crashes pricing.
- **Offload.** Recordings are copied off the droplet daily with rsync, for analysis on a laptop.

### 2. Replay harness

`apps/publisher/scripts/replay.mjs`:
- feeds a recording into `MarketData` in receipt-time order;
- runs `observeFeeds` on the recorded tick schedule;
- prints per-tick price, conf, venues used and fallbacks for two methodology versions;
- writes a diff summary and a comparison against the recorded live observations.

### 3. Determinism

- **Replay.** Two instances fed one recording must produce byte-identical observations.
- **Cross-publisher.** Live publishers receive streams at slightly different times, so they can
  legitimately disagree on a borderline trade. This is expected and bounded: each publisher's
  observation is independent, and the leader's median across publishers absorbs the disagreement.
- **Divergence test.** Replay the same recording with arrival times skewed by ±50 ms per event, for
  three simulated publishers. Their prices must stay within their confs of each other.

### 4. Trade-vs-book filter (`vwap` method only)

- **Timestamp.** Venue trade time when the venue provides it, otherwise local receipt time.
- **Book history.** `MarketData` keeps top-of-book history for at least `tradeBookToleranceMs`
  plus the window (already kept at 100 ms resolution for `mid` sampling).
- **Accept rule.** A trade counts only if its price is inside
  `[bid − slack, ask + slack]` for at least one book state within ±`tradeBookToleranceMs` of the
  trade time.
  - `slack = half-spread × tradeBookSlackSpreads`.
  - Ties go to accept: any matching book state is enough.
  - A trade with no book state within tolerance is rejected.
- **Volume floor.** If the accepted trades' notional in the window is below
  `vwapMinWindowNotional`, the venue uses the `mid` rule instead.
- **Residual attack.** An attacker can still print inside a book they moved first. That costs the
  book-moving capital (about $2k per 1% on KuCoin today). They can print at most `spread + slack`
  beyond that, and they still need 3 of 5 venues to move the median. So the filter turns "free wash
  prints" into "real buying on three venues". It does not make thin books deep.

### 5. Depth-aware conf and the venue minimum

Per venue `v`:
- `hs_v` = median half-spread;
- `gap_v` = |accepted-trade VWAP − mid| for `vwap` venues, 0 for `mid` venues;
- `depth_v` = notional within `depthBandBps` on the thinner side.

Venue handling:
- A venue with `depth_v < minDepthNotional` is dropped before the cross-venue step.
- The existing `minVenues` still applies after every filter (depth, deviation). Below it, the feed
  is omitted for that tick. This matches today's behavior: no signed price is better than a
  weakly supported one, and consumers already handle missing ticks.

Across the kept venues, where `P` is the aggregate price:
- `MAD = median(|P_v − P|)`
- `conf = max(median(hs_v), MAD, median(gap_v))`

### 6. Config fields and defaults

New optional committee-config fields: `tradeBookToleranceMs`, `tradeBookSlackSpreads`,
`vwapMinWindowNotional`, `depthBandBps`, `minDepthNotional`.
- **An absent field disables its filter**, so an existing (v1) config prices exactly as today.
- Committee config v2 sets them explicitly, from the replay results, and activates at a chosen
  tick.

### 7. Measurements to take (at least 3 days of recording)

- **Filter behavior:** how often each filter fires, per feed.
- **Cost to move each venue 1%:** reported per 4-hour bucket, which gives at least 18 samples per
  bucket over 3 days.
- **Cross-venue follow-through:**
  - the fraction of a 1% or larger Binance move that reaches the other venues within 5 s;
  - pre-registered threshold 70%;
  - reported only when at least 10 such moves occur, otherwise "insufficient events" and the
    recording continues.

## Open Questions

- Are the venue L2 depth streams allowed for this use (market-data terms, checked before mainnet)?
- If 1% moves stay rare, is a 0.5% move threshold an acceptable proxy for follow-through?

## Success Criteria

- **Replay matches live.** From the in-process recording, the harness reproduces the live
  publisher's recorded CKB/USDT observations within 1 bp on at least 99% of ticks.
- **The wash print fails.** A +5% print with $10 of volume on KuCoin leaves the aggregate unchanged,
  in a unit test and on replayed data with the print injected.
- **A moved-book print costs what we claim.** Moving the KuCoin book then printing inside it moves
  that venue only by the book move plus at most `spread + slack`, and the aggregate by at most the
  bounded residual described above.
- **Small price change.** On recorded data, v2's CKB/USDT price differs from v1's by at most
  **v1's** conf on at least 99% of ticks.
- **Coverage kept.** v2 omits a feed on no more than 1% of ticks.
- **Cost measured.** The replay write-up reports the measured cost to move CKB/USDT 1% (unsigned).
  Signing it is left to approach C.

## Reviewer Concerns

### R2-1 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The trade-vs-book filter compares a trade's venue timestamp with book states whose history is kept at 100 ms resolution in local receipt time (section 4). Venue clocks and local clocks differ by clock offset plus network latency, and the doc does not say which clock indexes the book history or how the two are reconciled. A fixed offset larger than tradeBookToleranceMs would reject every trade from that venue, or match trades against the wrong book.

**Remedy**

> Say which clock the book history uses. Use venue time for the books too when the venue provides it, or measure and correct each venue's clock offset, and add a replay check that reports the offset distribution per venue.

### R2-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> The residual-attack claim and the moved-book success criterion assume that 3 of 5 venues must be moved. But the new minDepthNotional filter drops thin venues before the median, and on CKB Gate and KuCoin have only about $2.2k-$2.4k within 1%. With a meaningful threshold, the median may be taken over 3 venues, where moving 2 is enough.

**Remedy**

> State the attack cost as a function of the number of venues left after the depth filter, and bound or check minDepthNotional so that the claimed cost (and the minVenues setting) still holds with the venues that actually remain.

### R2-3 — completeness

**Problem**

> The disk-budget claim that the droplet 'stays under 60%' (3 GB of recordings plus ~5.5 GB mirror plus ~0.4 GB publisher, about 9 GB) never states the droplet's disk size, so it cannot be checked. The daily rsync offload also has no stated owner or failure behavior.

**Remedy**

> State the droplet disk size and the arithmetic behind the 60% figure, and say who runs the offload and that deletion under the cap proceeds even if the offload has not run.

### R2-4 — clarity

**Problem**

> The slack uses 'half-spread', but section 4 does not say whether that is the half-spread of the matching book state or the venue's median half-spread hs_v from section 5. The two can differ a lot on a book the attacker has just moved.

**Remedy**

> Define the slack's half-spread as the matching book state's half-spread or as hs_v, and note how the choice affects the residual bound.

### R2-5 — clarity

**Problem**

> The divergence test requires that the three simulated publishers' prices 'stay within their confs of each other' without saying which conf (each publisher's own, the minimum, or the maximum) or the pass rate over ticks.

**Remedy**

> Give the exact pass condition, for example |P_i - P_j| <= min(conf_i, conf_j) on 100% (or a stated share) of ticks.

### Prior finding evidence

**R1-4 → R2-1 (persisting)**

> Section 4 defines the timestamp source, book-history retention and ties, and section 3 adds the ±50 ms divergence test. But venue-time trades are still compared with receipt-time book states and no clock reconciliation is given.
