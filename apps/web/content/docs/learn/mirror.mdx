---
title: The mirror
description: Where signed updates are collected, checked and served.
---

Publishers run on a private network. A **mirror** is the public service between them and
applications, much like Pyth's Hermes.

## What it does

- **Collects** every finalized update from several publishers of each committee, over a live stream,
  with a backfill on start, on reconnect and every minute.
- **Verifies** each update before storing it: committee, set index, quorum signatures and every
  Merkle proof. It re-reads the committee cell from CKB every 30 seconds.
- **Keeps recent history**: 7 days by default (`retentionDays`), about 0.8 GB per day for 12 feeds
  at one-second ticks. Archive operators can keep everything.
- **Serves** the [Mirror API](/docs/reference/mirror-api) over HTTP and WebSocket, with rate limits.

## Why you don't have to trust it

Every update carries its own signatures. A mirror can be slow, down, or out of date, but it cannot
make your contract accept a price the committee didn't sign. The SDK verifies everything it gets, and
the chain verifies again.

For redundancy, give the SDK several mirror URLs; it tries them in order.

## Equivocation

If two different valid headers ever appear for the same committee and tick, the mirror keeps the
first as canonical, stores both, and serves them at `/v1/equivocations` as evidence. Publishers
refuse to sign twice, so this should never happen.
