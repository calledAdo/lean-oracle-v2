## 1. Observe

Each **publisher** runs a service that keeps live connections to the exchanges listed for each feed.
It samples the order books through each **tick** (one second for major assets, two seconds for CKB).

Every feed is a **native pair**. BTC/USDT is priced only from BTC/USDT markets, and BTC/USD only
from BTC/USD markets. Publishers never convert between currencies. If you need a pair that isn't
listed, combine feeds yourself, for example CKB/USD = CKB/USDT × USDT/USD.

## 2. Agree on a price

All publishers compute the price the same way:

1. Keep only exchanges whose connection is alive and whose order book is healthy: not crossed, a
   narrow spread, and real size at the top of the book.
2. Take each exchange's mid price, sampled every 100 ms through the tick.
3. Drop exchanges that sit too far from the median of the others.
4. Take the **median** across exchanges as the price. Its **confidence** is the larger of the typical
   half-spread and the typical distance between exchanges.
5. Keep a one-hour moving average (EMA) of the price and confidence.

Each feed needs at least two exchanges to agree, so no single exchange can set a price.

## 3. Sign

Publishers are grouped into **committees**: `majors` for BTC, ETH, SOL and USDT, and `ckb` for CKB
pairs. A committee is defined by a cell on CKB that holds its publishers' public keys.

Each tick, the committee puts every feed's price into one Merkle tree, and a **quorum** of publishers
signs its root. The quorum is more than two thirds of the committee (`⌊2n/3⌋ + 1`). One set of
signatures covers every feed in the tick, and a short Merkle proof picks out one feed.

## 4. Serve

A **mirror** collects the signed updates from the publishers, verifies them and serves them over
HTTP and WebSocket, including full history. See the [Mirror API](/docs/reference/mirror-api).

A mirror is only transport. It cannot forge or alter a price, because every update carries its
signatures. Anyone can run one.

## 5. Submit

When your application needs a price, it fetches an update from a mirror and puts it in the witness of
a transaction that updates **your feed cell**. Your feed cell is a cell you created, with a unique
Type ID and a lock you chose.

## 6. Verify

The `price_feed_type` script on your feed cell accepts the new price only if:

- the Merkle proof places the price under the signed root;
- a quorum of the committee's current publishers signed that root;
- the new price is **newer** than the one the cell holds.

Your contract then reads the feed cell like any other cell dep.

## Feed cells

<CellLifecycle />

- A new feed cell holds no price (all zeros). A non-zero publish time proves the cell has had at least
  one verified update.
- Each update must be newer than the last, so old prices can never be replayed against your cell.
- The cell stores the committee it trusts. Your contract should check it, because anyone can create a
  cell for the same feed anchored to a different committee.
- The oracle never decides whether a price is fresh enough. That is up to your contract. See
  [Your responsibilities](/docs/build/responsibilities).
